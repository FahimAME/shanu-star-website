24K, 22K, 21K, 18K , What Do These Numbers Really Mean?24، 22، 21، 18 قيراط، ما معنى هذه الأرقام فعلاً؟

6 min read · Shanu Star Journal

Walk through Dubai Gold Souq on any afternoon and you'll see numbers everywhere: 24K, 22K, 21K, 18K. They appear on jewellery tags, bullion certificates, electronic displays, even tiny stamps inside rings. For first-time buyers, these numbers can feel like a code that everyone but you understands.

The good news is that the system is simple once you know it. Here's everything you need.

What "karat" actually measures

A karat (spelled "carat" outside the gold context) is a unit of gold purity. Pure gold, with nothing added, is 24 karat. That's the maximum, the reference point. Every other karat number tells you the proportion of pure gold mixed with other metals.

The math is straightforward, every karat number is divided by 24 to give you the percentage of gold:

  • 24K = 24/24 = 99.9%+ pure gold (the purest form sold commercially)
  • 22K = 22/24 = 91.6% pure gold (mostly used in Indian and Bahraini jewellery)
  • 21K = 21/24 = 87.5% pure gold (common in Arabic jewellery designs)
  • 18K = 18/24 = 75% pure gold (popular in European designs and everyday jewellery)

Why pure gold isn't always best

It sounds counterintuitive, but pure gold is actually too soft for everyday wear. A 24K ring would bend out of shape within months. The other metals mixed into 22K, 21K, and 18K gold aren't impurities, they're deliberate additions called alloys that give the gold strength and durability.

This is why jewellers in the UAE use specific karats for specific purposes:

  • 24K is for investment bullion, gold bars and coins that sit safely in a vault, not on your finger
  • 22K is the most widely chosen karat for jewellery, rich yellow colour, durable enough for everyday wear, with a very high gold content
  • 21K is widely used in Gulf jewellery, slightly more durable than 22K with a near-identical appearance
  • 18K is preferred for fine jewellery and stone-set pieces, with excellent durability for daily wear, secure stone settings, and a slightly lighter golden tone that suits modern designs

How to read a hallmark

Every legitimate gold piece sold in the UAE must be hallmarked, a small stamp confirming its purity. On bullion bars, look at the front face for stamps like:

  • "999.9" or "9999", this is 24K, expressed as parts per thousand
  • "916", this is 22K (91.6% pure)
  • "875", this is 21K
  • "750", this is 18K

On jewellery, the karat is often stamped inside the band (for rings), on the clasp (for necklaces), or on a small tag attached to the piece. If you can't see a stamp, ask the seller to show you. A reputable dealer will point it out without hesitation.

What this means for your money

Here's the bottom line: karat directly affects price per gram. A 1 gram piece of 24K gold costs more than 1 gram of 22K gold, simply because there's more gold inside it.

At today's rates, the per-gram difference between 24K and 22K is roughly 8 percent. For a 50 gram piece, that's hundreds of dirhams. When you compare prices at different shops, always make sure you're comparing the same karat. A "cheaper" 21K bracelet is not actually cheaper than a 22K one if you're paying the same per-gram rate.

One more thing: VAT

In the UAE, investment-grade gold (99.5% pure or higher) is VAT-free. That means 24K bullion bars and coins carry zero VAT. Jewellery, including 22K and 21K, is subject to 5% VAT on the making charges (not the gold itself). This is one of the practical reasons many investors choose pure bullion over jewellery for wealth preservation.

Bullion vs Jewellery: Which Should a First-Time Investor Buy?سبائك أم مجوهرات: أيهما يشتري المستثمر للمرة الأولى؟

8 min read · Shanu Star Journal

This is the single most common question we get from first-time buyers, and the honest answer might surprise you. Both bullion and jewellery have real value, but when the goal is investment, the path is much clearer than most people realise.

Our honest take: If you are buying gold primarily as an investment or wealth-preservation asset, bullion should be your first priority. It is purer, more cost-efficient per gram, easier to resell at predictable rates, and fully VAT-exempt in the UAE. Bullion is the cleanest, most efficient way to convert your money into gold.

But this does not make jewellery a lesser choice. Jewellery serves a different purpose entirely. It carries cultural value, emotional weight, the joy of being worn, and the meaning of being gifted. Many of our most valued customers buy both, bullion for their savings, jewellery for their lives. There is no wrong answer here, only the answer that matches what you actually want gold to do for you.

The numbers tell the story

Let's compare two purchases of equivalent gold content, a 100 gram 24K bullion bar versus a 100 gram 22K jewellery necklace. Both contain similar amounts of pure gold, but the price you pay tells very different stories.

The bullion bar carries a small premium above the spot gold price, typically 1 to 2 percent for a 100 gram bar from a major mint like PAMP or Valcambi. There is zero VAT because investment-grade gold (99.5% purity or higher) is VAT-exempt in the UAE. When you decide to sell, the buyback price is very close to spot, you lose only the dealer's small spread.

The jewellery necklace is different. You pay for the gold content (at 91.6% purity for 22K), plus making charges that can range from 10 percent to 40 percent depending on design complexity, plus 5 percent VAT on those making charges. When you sell that necklace back, the making charges vanish, you receive only the gold value at the day's rate.

What you keep, what you lose

The fundamental difference is this: with bullion, almost every dirham you spend stays in the gold. With jewellery, a significant portion goes into craftsmanship that has emotional value but no resale value.

This isn't a flaw of jewellery, it's the nature of it. When you buy a beautiful necklace, you're paying the goldsmith for hours of skilled work, design, and finishing. That work has real value while you wear and enjoy the piece. But on a pure investment basis, it doesn't carry forward.

Liquidity matters more than people realise

"Liquidity" means how quickly and easily you can convert something back to cash without losing value. Bullion is one of the most liquid assets on earth. A PAMP or Valcambi bar with its assay card can be sold at any reputable dealer in minutes, at a price you can verify against global spot prices online.

Jewellery is also liquid, every gold shop in the souq will buy gold jewellery. But the price you receive will be based purely on weight and karat, the design is not factored in. For investment exit purposes, bullion is simply faster and more predictable.

When jewellery is the right choice

None of this means jewellery is a poor purchase. There are excellent reasons to choose it:

  • Wearable value: You enjoy the piece every day. Bullion sits in a vault.
  • Cultural and gift significance: Gold jewellery sits at the heart of both Arab and Indian cultures, woven into family heritage, weddings, Eid, Diwali, wedding specials, festivals, and celebrations across generations. It is one of the most meaningful gifts you can give or receive in the Gulf and South Asian communities.
  • Hedge plus utility: You're still holding gold. The value of the metal itself doesn't disappear.
  • Heirloom value: A well-made piece can pass through generations carrying both monetary and sentimental worth.

A practical first-time strategy

Many of our customers do both, and we think this is a sensible approach. Allocate the majority of your gold budget to bullion (a 50g or 100g bar from a recognised mint is a great starting point), and use a smaller portion for one piece of meaningful jewellery, something you'll genuinely wear or gift.

This gives you the investment efficiency of bullion combined with the practical and cultural value of jewellery. Over time, you can add to your bullion holdings as your savings grow, while building a small jewellery collection at a comfortable pace.

How to Verify Authenticity Before You Payكيف تتحقق من الأصالة قبل أن تدفع

5 min read · Shanu Star Journal

Counterfeit gold bullion is rare in Dubai's regulated market, far rarer than in many other global gold trading centres. That's because the UAE has strict laws, the Dubai Multi Commodities Centre (DMCC) actively monitors the industry, and most established dealers are members of the Dubai Jewellery Group, all of which makes deliberate fraud unusual.

That said, "rare" is not "impossible." And whether you're buying a small bar or a multi-kilogram order, the five minutes you spend verifying before payment is the cheapest insurance you'll ever buy. Here's exactly what to check.

1. The assay card or certificate

Every legitimate bullion bar from a major mint comes sealed inside a tamper-evident card called an assay card. This card is not optional, it's the manufacturer's certification that the bar is what it claims to be.

The card should contain:

  • The mint's name and official logo (PAMP, Valcambi, Auriz, BTC, Emirates Gold, Swiss Gold, and so on)
  • The bar's exact weight (e.g., 100g, 1oz, 50g)
  • The purity stamp (999.9 for 24K)
  • A unique serial number
  • The signature or seal of the assayer (the chemist who verified the purity)
  • A QR code (where provided by the mint) that you can scan with your phone for official verification on the manufacturer's website

The assay card should be sealed shut. If it's been opened, ask why. There are legitimate reasons (a previous owner inspected the bar), but it should be disclosed.

2. The serial number match

The serial number printed on the assay card must match the number engraved on the bar itself. Compare them, character by character. Mints are extremely careful about this match, any discrepancy is a red flag.

For PAMP bars in particular, modern issues include a security feature called Veriscan, a microscopic fingerprint of the bar that can be verified through PAMP's official app. This is a high-confidence verification step worth doing for larger purchases.

3. The weight check

Any reputable dealer will have a precision scale at the counter and will weigh the bar in front of you. If they don't offer, ask. The weight should match the stated weight on both the bar and the assay card, to the gram.

For very small bars (1g, 2g, 5g), the assay card has a window that lets you see the bar without breaking the seal. The bar should fill the window completely, no gaps, no padding behind it.

4. The dealer's accreditation

This is the single most important check, more important than any individual bar inspection. Before you buy from any dealer in Dubai, ask one question:

"Are you a licensed Dealer in Precious Metals and Stones (DPMS), registered with the UAE Ministry of Economy?"

A legitimate dealer will answer yes immediately and may even show you their license. They will also be registered on the UAE Financial Intelligence Unit's goAML platform, which is a federal requirement for all gold dealers above certain transaction thresholds. If a dealer hesitates, deflects, or doesn't know what these terms mean, walk out.

5. The source of supply

For high-value purchases, ask where the bullion was sourced. Reputable dealers source through authorised distributors who themselves work with mints holding LBMA (London Bullion Market Association) accreditation. The LBMA Good Delivery List is the gold standard, no pun intended, for refinery quality globally.

You don't need to memorise the list. You just need to recognise that the dealer can answer the question. "Our PAMP bars come through their UAE-authorised distributor, and PAMP is on the LBMA Good Delivery List" is the kind of answer you want to hear.

What you don't need to do

You don't need to be an expert metallurgist or carry test acid to a gold shop. The combination of assay card + serial match + weight check + dealer accreditation covers 99% of risk. The remaining 1% is handled by mint-level security features like Veriscan, which you can use for peace of mind on large purchases.

Understanding Pricingفهم التسعير

Spot Price, DJG Rate, Premium , The Three Numbers Every Buyer Should Knowالسعر الفوري ومعدل DJG والعلاوة، الأرقام الثلاثة التي يجب أن يعرفها كل مشترٍ

7 min read · Shanu Star Journal

Walk into our shop and you'll see a digital board displaying the day's rates. If you compare these to global market quotes elsewhere, you may see slightly different numbers, but none of them are wrong. They are simply measuring slightly different things.

Once you understand the three core numbers, you'll understand gold pricing better than 90% of casual buyers.

Number 1: Spot Price

The spot price is the global benchmark price of gold, traded second-by-second on international markets. It's quoted in US dollars per troy ounce (a troy ounce equals 31.1035 grams).

The spot price reflects pure gold, no premium, no fabrication, no delivery, just the abstract price of the metal itself. It's set by enormous volumes of trading between banks, hedge funds, central banks, and mining companies on the London Bullion Market and similar exchanges.

Spot price moves constantly. In a single day, it can fluctuate by 1 to 2 percent, sometimes more. Trading volumes are enormous and global, which is why the price never stays still for long.

What this means for you: Spot price is the foundation, the absolute lowest theoretical price gold can have. But you can't actually buy a gram at spot price, because no one is selling gold without some markup to cover their costs and profit. Spot is the reference point, not the buying price.

Number 2: DJG Rate

The Dubai Jewellery Group (DJG) rate is the official souq rate published three times daily by the DJG, the trade association of UAE jewellers. It's the standard reference for retail gold pricing in the UAE.

DJG quotes prices in UAE Dirhams per gram, for the four common karats:

  • 24K (pure investment gold)
  • 22K (traditional jewellery)
  • 21K (Gulf jewellery)
  • 18K (fine jewellery and stone-set pieces)

The DJG rate takes the global spot price, converts it to AED, and adjusts for the local market. It's the closest thing to a "fair retail price" you'll find in Dubai. Almost every reputable dealer in the souq, including us, uses the DJG rate as the baseline for their pricing.

What this means for you: When comparing dealers, ask "Is this priced at the DJG rate?" A dealer charging significantly above DJG is overpricing. A dealer pricing below DJG either has very thin margins, or there's something else going on, ask why.

Number 3: Premium

The premium is the additional amount above spot or DJG that you pay for a specific product. It covers fabrication, packaging, certification, distribution, and the dealer's margin.

Premiums vary based on several factors:

  • Size: Smaller bars have higher premiums per gram. A 1 gram bar might carry a 5 to 8 percent premium. A 1 kilogram bar might carry only 1 to 2 percent. The reason is simple, the cost of making a 1g bar is similar to making a 100g bar, but spread over far less metal.
  • Brand: A PAMP or Valcambi bar carries a higher premium than a generic bar, because of brand recognition, security features, and tighter quality control. For investment, this premium is often worth it, you'll get more back at resale.
  • Form: Cast bars (poured into molds) usually carry lower premiums than minted bars (pressed under high pressure with detailed designs). Coins generally carry the highest premiums of all.
  • Demand: During periods of high gold demand or supply disruption, premiums rise. We've seen them double during major global events.

The buyback gap

Here's a question we get often: "Why is the price you offer to buy my gold lower than the price you sell it for?"

The honest answer is that any dealer needs to operate within a small spread. The buy price (what we pay you for your gold) and the sell price (what you pay us) differ by typically 2 to 5 percent for bullion bars, more for jewellery (because of the making charges lost on resale). This spread covers our operating costs and allows us to stay in business serving you and other customers.

When you evaluate a dealer, look at the size of this spread. A reputable dealer will be transparent about it. An evasive answer is a warning.

Putting it together: a real example

Let's say today's spot price is USD 2,000 per ounce. That converts to roughly AED 236 per gram of pure (24K) gold.

The DJG might publish today's 24K rate at AED 240 per gram, slightly above spot to reflect retail market conditions.

A 50g PAMP bar might be priced at AED 244 per gram, the DJG rate plus a small premium for the brand and size.

If you buy that 50g bar, you pay 50 × 244 = AED 12,200. If you sold it back the same day, you might receive 50 × 236 = AED 11,800. That AED 400 difference, just over 3 percent, is the spread.

That's how the three numbers work together. Once you see them clearly, you can compare offers, evaluate dealers, and time your purchases with much more confidence.

A Walk Through Dubai Gold Souq , An Insider Guideجولة في سوق الذهب بدبي، دليل من الداخل

10 min read · Shanu Star Journal

Dubai Gold Souq sits in Deira, the old commercial heart of the city, just across the creek from the modern downtown. It's one of the largest concentrated gold markets in the world, with an estimated ten tonnes of gold on display at any given moment, more than the total reserves of many small countries.

For visitors and first-time buyers, the souq can feel both magical and confusing. The lanes are narrow, every window glitters, and shopkeepers are warm and persuasive. Here's how to enjoy the experience while making smart decisions.

The geography, briefly

The souq is essentially a covered grid of pedestrian lanes, anchored by the famous wooden ornamental archway. The main lanes run east-west and are lined with both jewellery showrooms and dedicated bullion dealers. Side alleys lead to smaller specialty shops.

The general organisation has emerged over decades, larger jewellery showrooms tend to occupy the most visible front-row positions on the main lanes, while specialised bullion dealers, refiners' offices, and smaller traditional jewellers are often found a row or two back, behind or beside the main streets. Once you know to look, you'll find them.

Our shop is located behind the Gold Center Building at Gate 3, in this exact pattern, a quieter spot, but with deep specialisation in bullion and serious buyers.

What to expect when you arrive

The souq is busy from late morning until late evening, with a quieter period in the early afternoon (Friday hours start later, after midday prayers). Most shops are open from around 10am to 10pm.

You'll be approached by friendly shopkeepers offering to show you their pieces. This is normal, polite, and part of the souq culture. There's no obligation to buy. Browsing is welcome, expected, and enjoyable. Take your time.

Bring water (it's warm), comfortable shoes (the lanes are long), and if you plan to make a serious purchase, bring your Emirates ID or passport regardless of transaction size, it's a good practice and signals that you understand the process.

The different types of shops

Not every shop in the souq is doing the same thing. Understanding the categories helps you head toward the right kind of shop for what you want:

  • Large jewellery showrooms: Bright, branded, often part of regional chains. Great for finished jewellery, gift sets, and bridal pieces. Less ideal for raw bullion investment.
  • Specialist bullion dealers: Usually smaller, more focused. Their windows show gold bars, coins, and assay cards rather than necklaces. This is where you go for investment-grade gold.
  • Traditional jewellers: Family businesses, often with deep specialty in regional designs (Arabic, Indian, Persian). Excellent for cultural pieces, custom work, and personal relationships.
  • Refiners' showrooms: A handful of operations sell directly from refineries (like Emirates Gold). These can be excellent for buyers who want to see the supply chain shortened.

Questions worth asking, in any shop

You're under no obligation to buy from the first shop you visit, or even from the most persuasive one. The right shop for you is the one whose answers to these questions you trust:

  • "What's today's rate per gram for the karat I'm looking at?" A confident answer with reference to DJG rates is a good sign.
  • "Can I see the assay card or hallmark?" For bullion, the card should be sealed and the dealer should walk you through it. For jewellery, the hallmark should be clearly stamped.
  • "Do you offer buyback?" Most reputable dealers do. The presence of a buyback service is a strong indicator of confidence in their product.

What to enjoy along the way

The souq isn't just a market, it's a cultural experience. Whether or not you buy anything, take a moment to:

  • Look up, the carved wooden roofs are beautiful and architecturally distinctive
  • Visit the world's largest gold ring on display in the souq, an actual Guinness Record holder
  • Wander into the spice souq, just a few minutes' walk away, the two markets complement each other perfectly
  • Stop for Arabic coffee at one of the small cafés, it's a souq tradition, often offered by shopkeepers themselves

Why this matters

Dubai Gold Souq has been a centre of regional gold trade for generations. It survived through wars, economic changes, and the rise of online trading because it offers something the internet cannot, the chance to see, hold, and verify gold in person, alongside genuine human conversation with people who have spent decades in the trade.

If you come prepared with the knowledge of what to look for and what to ask, you'll find the souq is one of the most efficient and trustworthy gold markets in the world. It's a place built on relationships and reputation, which means the better you understand it, the better it works for you.

Compliance & Safetyالامتثال والأمان

LBMA, DMCC, DPMS , The Acronyms That Protect YouLBMA و DMCC و DPMS، الاختصارات التي تحميك

6 min read · Shanu Star Journal

Gold is one of the most regulated commodities in the world, especially when it crosses international borders. The UAE specifically has built a sophisticated compliance framework over the last decade, with the result that Dubai is now one of the most trusted gold trading hubs globally.

Three acronyms appear constantly in this world, and understanding them gives you a simple, powerful test for whether any dealer you're considering is operating legitimately.

LBMA: The London Bullion Market Association

The LBMA is an international standard-setting body for gold and silver markets. Headquartered in London, it has been the backbone of global precious metals trading since 1987.

Its most important function for ordinary buyers is the Good Delivery List, a list of refineries worldwide whose gold bars meet strict purity, weight, and quality standards. To be on the list, a refinery must pass rigorous audits on:

  • The chemical purity of its bars (99.5% minimum, usually 99.99%)
  • Manufacturing precision (exact weights, consistent dimensions, secure marking)
  • Responsible sourcing of the raw gold (no conflict gold, no human rights abuses)
  • Financial stability and ongoing compliance

When you see "LBMA Good Delivery" on a refinery, it means that refinery's bars are accepted at any major bank vault or trading house anywhere in the world, without re-assay. That's the highest possible level of trust in the global gold market.

Refineries that supply the brands we carry, like PAMP Suisse, Valcambi, Royal Mint, Perth Mint, and others, are all on the LBMA Good Delivery List. This is the foundation of trust in their product.

DMCC: Dubai Multi Commodities Centre

DMCC is the UAE's free zone authority overseeing commodities trade, including gold and other precious metals. Established in 2002, it has built Dubai's position as a major precious metals hub through both infrastructure (vaults, refineries, trading platforms) and regulation.

DMCC's most relevant role for buyers is its Rules for Risk-Based Due Diligence in the Gold Supply Chain. This is a mandatory framework for all gold market participants in DMCC, modelled on the OECD's international guidance. It requires every link in the chain, from miner to refinery to dealer, to identify and manage risks like conflict gold, money laundering, and human rights abuses.

Practically, this means a gold bar that has passed through a DMCC-compliant supply chain has been checked at multiple points. The DMCC framework is recognised internationally, including by the LBMA, which gives Dubai-traded gold global credibility.

DPMS: Dealer in Precious Metals and Stones

This is the category every legitimate gold dealer in the UAE falls into. The DPMS designation comes from federal law and means a business that buys or sells gold, silver, platinum, diamonds, or other precious materials as part of its commercial activity.

DPMS is regulated by the UAE Ministry of Economy, and dealers must meet specific obligations under Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 and Cabinet Resolution No. 134 of 2025:

  • Customer Due Diligence (CDD) for transactions at or above AED 55,000
  • Identity verification, Emirates ID or passport, for relevant transactions
  • Source of funds verification for higher-value or higher-risk transactions
  • Filing the DPMSR (Dealers in Precious Metals and Stones Report) within two weeks of qualifying transactions, via the FIU's goAML platform
  • Sanctions screening against OFAC, UN, EU, UAE local, and UKHMT terrorism lists
  • Record retention for a minimum of five years
  • Staff training in AML/CFT/CPF (counter-proliferation financing)

At Shanu Star Jewellery, we operate fully within this DPMS framework. Our Compliance Officer, Mohammed Fahad, maintains the policies and procedures, our records are kept in line with the regulations, and our staff are trained accordingly.

The 30-second test

Here's the simplest way to use this knowledge. When you're considering buying from any UAE gold dealer, ask them this single question:

"Are you a registered DPMS dealer, and do your bullion products come from LBMA Good Delivery refineries?"

A legitimate dealer will answer "yes" without hesitation, and will likely be able to elaborate, explaining their compliance procedures, naming their refinery sources, or even showing you their license. If a dealer is confused by the question, deflects, or tries to change the subject, that's all the information you need.

This test is genuinely effective because legitimate dealers spend significant resources on compliance. They're proud of it, because it's what allows them to operate professionally and stand behind their products. They won't be irritated by the question, they'll be reassured that you understand the framework they work within.

Why this all exists

Gold has unique properties that make it attractive both for legitimate commerce and for criminal misuse. It's highly valuable, easy to transport, easy to hide, and easy to convert between forms. Throughout history, it has been used to move illicit wealth across borders, to fund conflict, and to evade financial systems.

The LBMA, DMCC, DPMS, and similar frameworks exist to prevent this. They create transparency in supply chains, traceability of transactions, and accountability for dealers. The result is that buying gold in Dubai today is dramatically safer and more transparent than at almost any point in history.

For you as a buyer, this means three things. First, your gold is real and traceable. Second, your dealer is accountable, regulated, and auditable. Third, the wider system protects you from inadvertently participating in something illegal. That's a quietly remarkable achievement, and worth understanding.
